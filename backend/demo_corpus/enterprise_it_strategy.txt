ENTERPRISE IT STRATEGY — LEADERSHIP REFERENCE GUIDE

DIGITAL TRANSFORMATION
Digital transformation is the process of integrating digital technology into all business areas, fundamentally changing how an organization operates and delivers value. It requires cultural change, new skill sets, and rethinking legacy processes. Key pillars: cloud migration, data-driven decision-making, automation, and customer experience redesign. McKinsey reports that 70% of digital transformations fail — the most common causes are lack of leadership alignment, insufficient change management, and technology-first (rather than outcome-first) approaches.

CLOUD STRATEGY: BUILD VS BUY VS RENT
The classic build-buy-rent decision is now cloud-native: IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) provides raw compute/storage (AWS EC2, Azure VMs), PaaS (Platform as a Service) provides managed runtime environments (AWS Elastic Beanstalk, Google App Engine), SaaS (Software as a Service) delivers fully managed applications (Salesforce, ServiceNow). Best practice: default to SaaS for commodity functions (email, HR, CRM), use PaaS for custom applications, reserve IaaS for workloads with unique infrastructure requirements.

IT GOVERNANCE AND ITIL
ITIL (IT Infrastructure Library) is the most widely adopted IT service management framework. ITIL 4 introduces the Service Value System (SVS), emphasizing value co-creation, agility, and DevOps integration. Core practices: incident management (restore service fast), problem management (eliminate root causes), change enablement (reduce change risk), and service desk (single point of contact). KPIs: MTTR (Mean Time to Restore), MTTD (Mean Time to Detect), change success rate, first contact resolution.

CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORKS
NIST CSF (Cybersecurity Framework) organizes security activities around five functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. ISO 27001 provides a systematic approach to information security management (ISMS). Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) assumes no implicit trust — every access request is verified regardless of network location. Key controls: MFA, least-privilege access, network segmentation, endpoint detection and response (EDR), SIEM (Security Information and Event Management).

AI ADOPTION IN THE ENTERPRISE
Enterprise AI adoption follows a maturity curve: (1) Experimentation — isolated pilots with no integration, (2) Operationalization — production deployments with MLOps, (3) Transformation — AI embedded in core business processes. Key adoption barriers: data quality, talent gap, regulatory uncertainty, and change management. High-ROI use cases: predictive maintenance, demand forecasting, intelligent document processing, IT operations automation (AIOps), and customer service augmentation.

TOTAL COST OF OWNERSHIP (TCO)
TCO analysis captures all direct and indirect costs over a system's lifecycle: acquisition, implementation, integration, training, maintenance, support, and decommissioning. Cloud TCO models differ from on-premise: OPEX replaces CAPEX, but hidden costs (data egress, reserved instance commitments, idle resources) can inflate bills. FinOps (Cloud Financial Management) is the discipline of optimizing cloud spend through tagging, rightsizing, and reserved capacity planning.

DATA GOVERNANCE
Data governance defines ownership, quality standards, access controls, and lifecycle management for enterprise data assets. Key roles: Chief Data Officer (CDO), Data Stewards, Data Architects. Frameworks: DAMA-DMBOK (Data Management Body of Knowledge). Regulatory compliance drivers: GDPR (EU), CCPA (California), HIPAA (healthcare), SOX (financial reporting). Master Data Management (MDM) creates a single source of truth for critical entities (customers, products, suppliers).

VENDOR MANAGEMENT
Strategic vendor management evaluates suppliers across technical capability, financial stability, cultural fit, and SLA performance. Key tools: RFP (Request for Proposal), SLA (Service Level Agreement), SOW (Statement of Work), and vendor scorecards. Concentration risk — over-dependence on a single vendor — is a growing concern as cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) consolidate market share. Multi-cloud and hybrid strategies mitigate lock-in.

AGILE AND DEVOPS AT SCALE
Scaling agile beyond a single team requires frameworks: SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework), LeSS (Large-Scale Scrum), or Spotify Model. DevOps extends agile to operations: CI/CD pipelines (continuous integration, continuous deployment), infrastructure as code (Terraform, Ansible), and site reliability engineering (SRE). DORA metrics (Deployment Frequency, Lead Time for Changes, Change Failure Rate, MTTR) are the industry standard for measuring DevOps performance.

ENTERPRISE ARCHITECTURE
Enterprise Architecture (EA) aligns business strategy with IT capabilities. The Zachman Framework and TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) are dominant methodologies. EA domains: Business Architecture (processes, org structure), Data Architecture (data models, flows), Application Architecture (systems, integrations), Technology Architecture (infrastructure, platforms). EA governance prevents shadow IT and ensures technology investments support strategic objectives.
